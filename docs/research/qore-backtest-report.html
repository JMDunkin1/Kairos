Why did the supply filter match the fallback?

It avoided every recent gas entry and kept the allocation in the index funds. That helped in this period, but the filter underperformed the fallback in 2024.

Supply growth

Production outgrows LNG exports

Subtract year-over-year LNG-export growth from year-over-year dry-production growth. A value above 0 Bcf/day indicates extra supply relative to this one source of demand.

Skip the gas purchase only when both conditions are true.

Equality does not block a purchase. This measures part of the gas balance; it does not capture every source of supply and demand.

How we checked whether different numbers were better

We locked a nine-setting comparison from 2021–2023 features: storage cutoffs of 0%, 14.90%, and 18.10%; flow cutoffs of 0, 2.39, and 2.75 Bcf/day. Reports were counted once, rather than repeatedly counting the same monthly data.

The best-looking training setting returned 58.32%, compared with 48.67% for zero/zero. But only two independent trading episodes distinguished the settings. Its family-adjusted p-value was 0.177, and its 90% simultaneous confidence range included no improvement. None met the predeclared requirements for replacing the zero boundaries.

We used 2,000 paired ten-session block resamples to account for clustered returns and the nine settings tested. Training nominated the rule before the 2024 check; 2025 was reporting-only. No 2026 outcomes, including the recent month, selected these numbers.

Conclusion: there is insufficient evidence for a statistically best cutoff. Zero/zero remains the understandable economic default chosen for the paper experiment, not a mathematically proven optimum.

What each change means, in plain English
  • Remove automatic shorts: keep the heat-driven buys, then return the allocation to the fallback instead of automatically betting on a price decline.
  • Use UNG price signals: measure the rally in the ETF actually traded, instead of the separate NG=F futures price series.
  • Forecast revisions: require a broad increase in predicted cooling demand, comparing forecasts for the same weather date.
  • Storage filter: skip heat-driven buys when released storage is above its seasonal five-year comparison.
  • Supply filter: skip those buys when storage is above that comparison and growth in gas production exceeds growth in LNG exports. This is only part of the overall gas balance.
  • Index fallback: the configured 80% VOO / 20% QQQM basket for deployable index capital, with a 2% portfolio cash reserve.
How the numbers were checked
  • The outage replay begins with actual July 24 holdings and $97,830.27. Every variant reaches the same $100,033.58 by August 26, before the recent gas entries.
  • The portfolio simulation includes fallback gains, overnight holdings, trading costs, cash reserve, and the existing rebalance rules. Baseline, elevated, and stress costs were evaluated.
  • Supply inputs came from 70 archived EIA releases, using estimates available at the time. Publication dates and correction notices constrain when each can be used.
  • Checks reproduced 585 historical summer targets and 1,316 all-year allocations. An independent cash/share ledger reconciled the recent variants.
  • Tests checked that future prices, forecasts, and unreleased storage cannot alter earlier decisions. An independent subagent reviewed the calculations.
  • Winter keeps its original, later fitting dates. The full all-year 2025 portfolio is therefore not wholly held out. The protected 2017–2019 reforecast holdout was not opened.
