# All-Year Natural-Gas Strategy Contract

## Scope

`ngas-all-year-beta` is the natural-gas subsystem's only public strategy. It is a deterministic daily selector over two internal component ledgers:

1. use the material Summer Alpha row;
2. otherwise use the material Winter Alpha row;
3. otherwise use the shared `US-INDEX-BASKET` fallback target.

A component row is material when it has a non-index thesis, non-zero gas position, or an explicit index-to-cash allocation change. If summer and winter are both material on the same date, artifact generation fails instead of choosing silently. The all-year layer selects targets first and then recomputes the complete UNG/VOO/QQQM execution ledger, so component-local costs cannot decide which lane wins. It adds no threshold search, sizing search, or holdout-driven selection.

## Economic thesis

The winter engine tests the observation that sufficiently broad cold forecasts across the central and eastern United States can raise expected heating demand and natural-gas prices. Longer-lead cold confirmation can support a gas-follow position; as the event approaches or the market overreacts, the engine can use weather resolution and same-direction price behavior for a reversion/fade decision.

The summer engine buys a gas overlay for fresh, multi-model heat forecasts and returns that allocation to the index fallback when the heat window ends. It does not automatically short a subsequent rally. Before a Summer gas purchase, a supply filter requires valid released storage and EIA STEO context and suppresses the purchase when both storage is above its seasonal comparison and year-over-year dry-production growth exceeds year-over-year LNG-export growth. Winter rules are unchanged. Cold Summer forecasts remain an opposing-strength veto and diagnostic only; they cannot authorize a Summer gas short.

The supply rule is `storageSurplusPct > 0 && supplyGrowthLessLngGrowthBcfd > 0`, with strict inequalities. Storage surplus is `100 * (releasedStorageBcf / seasonalAverageBcf - 1)` using the existing same-week comparison over the previous five years (at least three matching reports). Flow growth is `(productionThisYear - productionPriorYear) - (LNGThisYear - LNGPriorYear)` in Bcf/d, comparing the latest completed observation month and its prior-year counterpart within the same released STEO workbook. This is a partial supply-pressure proxy, not a complete gas balance.

Zero/zero is an economic sign convention, not a statistically identified optimum. The frozen nine-cell threshold study used unique 2021–2023 released supply features to define its grid, then a paired 2,000-draw, ten-session block bootstrap with family-adjusted uncertainty to nominate a threshold. No nonzero choice met the declared replacement requirements; the training-best alternative had only two independent affected episodes and a family-adjusted p-value of 0.177. The 2024 check and 2025 reporting period did not select thresholds, and no 2026 data entered fitting. Earlier development had already inspected these historical years. See `docs/research/ngas-supply-threshold-protocol.json` and `data/qore/research/ngas-supply-threshold-audit.json`. The operator chose this simpler paper policy; historical evidence does not establish that it is safer or more profitable than the previous rules or the fallback.

## Research contract

The summer and winter components retain their own train, validation, and holdout boundaries. Each component selects and determines its eligibility on its own declared train/validation period only. The all-year artifact records each source row's component split separately. Its public `split` is one calendar-wide partition: train ends at the earliest component train end, public holdout starts at the latest component holdout, and the intervening sessions are validation. A separate selection cutoff ends before the earliest component holdout. All-year return-based promotion gates and the selection bootstrap use only that chronological prefix; later composite returns are reporting-only at the all-year layer, while the component-eligibility gates retain each component's declared pre-holdout result. This ensures no component validation row is mislabeled as public holdout and no component holdout return enters the current all-year return-based gate calculation. It does not make those historical periods pristine after repeated strategy development.

All-year eligibility uses a centered circular block bootstrap of every chronological daily active return in that contiguous calendar-wide train/validation prefix versus the index fallback. The UI's Monte Carlo panel is explicitly a report-only full-calendar block-bootstrap diagnostic, not a forecast of live wealth or an independent proof of significance.

### Independent fixed-model reforecast lane

The NOAA GEFSv12 retrospective archive provides a separate, research-only check on the physical demand thesis over the UNG era before Kairos's 2021 forecast calendars. `scripts/build-gefs-v12-reforecast-calendar.mjs` reads only the bounded 2 m temperature messages needed for forecast hours 174, 180, 186, and 192; samples the same reviewed 18-location basket; compares the four-sample mean with the retained NASA POWER daily normal; hashes the NOAA index, every selected byte range, every normal payload, and every output record; and writes only beneath `.local/qore/research/` by default. Raw GRIB objects are not retained. Completed date shards can be authenticated and combined with `scripts/merge-gefs-v12-reforecast-shards.mjs`.

`scripts/evaluate-gefs-v12-reforecast.mjs` tests a frozen 12-cell family of simple hot-Summer/cold-Winter demand-follow rules in a 35% UNG-versus-cash sleeve with a 3.2 bp one-way cost. It selects on sessions through 2014, requires positive aggregate and individual-year 2015-2016 validation, at least 20 active validation sessions, positive validation Sharpe, and a family-adjusted block-bootstrap p-value no greater than 0.20, and only then permits the locked winner to be evaluated on 2017-2019. The holdout command cannot evaluate challenger rows. The source is a later-vintage fixed-model reforecast rather than forecasts historically available to an operator, and pre-2020 history cannot reproduce the current QQQM index basket, so this lane validates only a narrow weather/demand thesis. It cannot change the selected all-year artifact, satisfy production parity, or enable paper/live routing.

The 2026-07-22 control-member run completed all 3,865 planned records with no failures. Every candidate lost money through 2014. The train-selected row returned -3.28% in training and +3.01% in 2015-2016, but 2016 was negative and the full-family adjusted p-value was 0.6837. Validation failed, so the 2017-2019 holdout remains unevaluated. The narrow versioned result and binding digests live in `data/qore/research/gefs-v12-reforecast-audit.json`; the reconstructable sampled dataset stays ignored under `.local/qore/research/`.

Invoke the standalone runners directly so this research-only lane does not change the sealed `package.json` production-input digest:

```bash

node scripts/evaluate-gefs-v12-reforecast.mjs
node scripts/evaluate-gefs-v12-reforecast.mjs --reveal-holdout
node scripts/test-qore-gefs-v12-reforecast.mjs
```

### Price-exhaustion and storage-contradiction shadows

The price-exhaustion and storage-contradiction studies are separately sealed, historical research shadows bound to the legacy active all-year ledger. Price exhaustion tests whether a same-direction five-session `UNG` move greater than one normalized volatility unit should suppress gas exposure; storage contradiction tests whether a selected gas direction conflicts with a causally available EIA storage deviation greater than 10%. Both return released exposure to the index basket, include the full frozen sensitivity families and cost scenarios, and are explicitly ineligible for execution, promotion, live inference, broker handoffs, dashboard telemetry, and the public strategy.

Their historical improvements remain development-contaminated and fail important robustness checks: both lose incremental return in 2025, the storage result is concentrated in only 13 independent episodes, and the price rule fails its one-session-lag negative control. The versioned audits therefore retain the active legacy strategy and require a separately reviewed contract plus genuinely prospective evidence before either idea can be reconsidered.

### Extreme-heat treatment shadow

The extreme-heat study is another separate, historical research shadow bound to the legacy active all-year ledger. It tests 16 fixed ways to reduce, veto, delay, or condition selected `summer-heat-long` exposure when cooling-demand anomaly is at least 8F across at least eight extreme locations, with partial aggregate/count sensitivity checks. The retained location count is always precomputed at a fixed 8F per-location threshold, so the 6F/six-location and 10F/ten-location checks do not fully redefine that location threshold. Causal features are limited to selected-episode age, released EIA storage versus its seasonal average, and completed `NG=F` returns available by the forecast issue date. Released exposure returns to the index basket; two small short-fade rows remain research-only.

A blanket extreme-heat veto fails: it improves the through-2024 prefix by 2.16 daily-sum percentage points, loses 2.42 points in report-only 2025, and is 0.25 points worse over the complete ledger. The descriptive storage-deficit gate leads through 2024 by 8.67 points but also loses 2.42 points in 2025; its three-requested-rule family-adjusted block-10 p-value is 0.143, while the full 16-row sensitivity-family value is 0.401. The exact issue-session and three-session price gates add only 1.95 and 0.60 points through 2024, have fixed block-10 p-values of 0.364 and 0.480, fail leave-one-changed-year stability, and are outperformed by their one-extra-session-lag controls at +7.67 and +2.39 points. Across the three gates there are only 4–9 independent causal clusters after execution tails and the ten-session embargo.

The checked comparator ends 2026-07-14, so the supplied July 15–24 loss is context rather than a backfilled outcome. The audit independently replays causal EIA release timing and completed authoritative price sessions, but storage values remain current-vintage and the legacy hours-0 extreme regime has not been reproduced under the corrected four-sample Summer input contract. The focal issue-price gate fails closed on five weekend/holiday issues without an exact issue-date session; the three-session gate and a separately reported anticipatory-price sensitivity may end on the preceding completed session. Historical Summer lead-8-to-lead-7 revisions and point-in-time production vintages are unavailable and are not synthesized. The exact fresh-revision idea remains in the prospective `spatial-demand-revision-breadth-price-gate-v1` lane. The versioned audit is `data/qore/research/extreme-heat-shadow-audit.json`; see `docs/extreme-heat-shadow.md`.

### Overnight return decomposition

The overnight decomposition separates each legacy all-year session into close-to-next-open and open-to-close contributions, reconciles both legs to the selected-trades ledger, attributes them between `UNG` and the index basket, and compares fixed overnight-retention levels with the deployed `carry-100` policy. On the legacy baseline, reducing overnight exposure monotonically lowers full-period CAGR after the modeled extra turnover and costs; the selected adaptive challenger also fails all frozen holdout Sharpe, drawdown, and CAGR gates. This is reporting-only research and does not change the deployed policy.

## Research versus execution

Summer Alpha retains Yahoo's continuous `NG=F` proxy only for price confirmation, realized-move gates, and signal-volatility sizing. Every gas return used for Summer selection, Winter selection, and the all-year portfolio is Yahoo `UNG` ETF history. The all-year ledger therefore has one gas P&L contract: `UNG`, matching the Alpaca adapter.

The frozen `config/qore-research-execution.json` contract applies a causal daily timeline. Prior-close holdings earn the close-to-open move; current targets become effective at the split- and distribution-adjusted session open and earn only open-to-close returns. Any same-date close-in weather update used at that open comes from a frozen 00Z run assumed available before the New York session; the historical calendar does not store a separate publication timestamp. Summer and Winter storage logic uses the versioned EIA WNGSR release calendar, including published holiday changes and documented extraordinary delays, and admits a report only at a session open after its publication timestamp. Winter season drawdown uses September as its canonical accumulation boundary. A missing storage release-calendar row fails closed. NASA POWER actual meteorology carries a conservative three-calendar-day availability lag. Turnover is computed separately for UNG, VOO, and QQQM after drift and the broker's default 0.25% rebalance deadband. That deadband may suppress immaterial same-direction drift, but the shared policy always executes UNG entry, exit, direction-change, and target risk-reduction orders; it also carries paired asset reductions with any executable gross increase and forces reductions when the portfolio starts above its deployment envelope. The Alpaca reconciler applies the same rule again at its fresh-quote boundary and blocks if excess exposure cannot be reduced by a valid order. Exactly one frozen baseline cost scenario may rank candidates; elevated and stress costs are reporting-only and cannot be made selection-eligible without changing the versioned contract.

The separate `config/qore-overnight-risk-policy.json` contract tests whether Kairos should reduce UNG at the close and restore the ordinary target at the next open. Its 50-policy audit covers fixed retention, long-only and short-only flattening, weekend flattening, and lagged rolling-gap guards under baseline, elevated, and stress friction. Calendar years 2021–2023 train the overlay, 2024 validates it, and 2025 onward is a report-only retrospective period. The pre-2025 winner is the sole research recommendation; later comparisons cannot promote, reject, or replace it. The deployed policy remains `carry-100` until the recommended close-side execution path is separately reviewed and implemented. Reproduce the audit with `npm run research:overnight-risk`.

`carry-100` means no closing-auction or overnight-session order is added: the prior-close UNG holding owns the overnight move and the next causal target still starts at the regular-session open. Alternative auction or extended-hours routes remain research-only unless their own timestamped quote/fill evidence, broker entitlement, order cutoffs, and whole-share constraints are validated.

This is still an idealized once-per-session execution simulation. The live supervisor may reassess an updated target or drift intraday, while the daily Yahoo history cannot reconstruct those quote crossings or fills. Missing historical quotes and fills cannot be recreated, and future gaps, slippage, partial fills, market impact, and borrow availability remain live outcomes. NASA POWER actuals are not archived as immutable historical vintages, so later upstream revisions also cannot be removed retrospectively. ETF expenses and historical futures-roll tracking are already embedded in UNG adjusted prices; taxes remain account-specific and are excluded from the pretax research return.

Negative `UNG` targets require a margin/short-capable Alpaca account, explicit Kairos permission, current Alpaca shortability, and whole-share sizing. If those conditions fail, Kairos blocks the order rather than changing the strategy.

True `NG`, `MNG`, or `QG` execution remains out of scope until the project has reviewed contract-month selection, expiry and roll logic, margin and price-limit handling, and delivery-risk controls.

## Promotion standard

Treat the checked-in artifact as a research baseline. Paper routing is a prospective experiment of the current executable strategy.
